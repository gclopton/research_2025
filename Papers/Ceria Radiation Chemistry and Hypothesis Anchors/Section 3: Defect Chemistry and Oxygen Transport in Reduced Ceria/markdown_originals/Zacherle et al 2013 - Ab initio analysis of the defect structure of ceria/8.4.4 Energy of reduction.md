Discussion: The passage explains that reducing ceria (CeO$_2$) requires removing oxygen ions from the lattice and simultaneously generating electronic defects. These electronic defects are specifically identified as electron polarons, where electrons become localized on cerium sites.  

#### 1. How is the reduction reaction in ceria formally written?  
Discussion: The reduction reaction is shown as:  
  
$$2 \mathrm{Ce}_{\mathrm{Ce}}^{\times} + \mathrm{O}_{\mathrm{O}}^{\times} \;\rightleftharpoons\; \mathrm{V}_{\mathrm{O}}^{\ddot{}} + 2 \mathrm{Ce}_{\mathrm{Ce}}^{\prime} + \tfrac{1}{2} \mathrm{O}_2(\mathrm{g})$$  
  
Here, $\mathrm{Ce}_{\mathrm{Ce}}^{\times}$ denotes a regular (undisturbed) cerium site, $\mathrm{O}_{\mathrm{O}}^{\times}$ is a regular oxygen site, $\mathrm{V}_{\mathrm{O}}^{\ddot{}}$ is an oxygen vacancy, and $\mathrm{Ce}_{\mathrm{Ce}}^{\prime}$ represents a Ce$^{}3+$ site (indicative of electron polaron formation).  
  
#### 1. How is the reduction energy $\Delta E_{\text{Red}}$ calculated?  
Discussion: Based on the reaction equation, the total energy of the reduced system is compared with that of the original system plus half an oxygen molecule. Mathematically, the reduction energy is given by:  
  
$$\Delta E_{\mathrm{Red}} = E_{\mathrm{tot}, \mathrm{corr}}(\mathrm{V}_{\mathrm{O}}^*) + 2\,E_{\mathrm{tot}, \mathrm{corr}}(\mathrm{Ce}_{\mathrm{Ce}}^{\prime}) + \tfrac{1}{2} E_{\mathrm{tot}}(\mathrm{O}_2) - 3\,E_{\mathrm{tot}}(\mathrm{bulk})$$  
  
This expression takes into account the formation of an oxygen vacancy, two reduced Ce ions, and half an $\mathrm{O}_2$ molecule, compared with the original bulk state.  
  
#### 1. What correction is applied to account for the overbinding of the $\mathbf{O_2}$ molecule?  
Discussion: The Generalized Gradient Approximation (GGA) in density-functional theory is known to overbind the oxygen molecule, leading to an underestimation of the reaction energy. To correct this, the authors apply a correction term following the method proposed by Wang et al.$^{}58$. This ensures the computed reduction energy more accurately reflects experimental values.  
  
#### 1. What is the corrected reduction energy and how does it compare to experimental values?  
Discussion: After including the overbinding correction, the authors obtain a corrected reduction energy,  
  
$$\Delta E_{\mathrm{Red}}^{\mathrm{corr}} = 4.21 \,\mathrm{eV}.$$  
  
Experimentally, Tuller$^{}25$ reports a reduction enthalpy of $\Delta H_{\mathrm{Red}} = 4.67\,\mathrm{eV}$ for undoped ceria, which is about 10% higher but still considered very good agreement. Additionally, for 15% Sm-doped ceria, Chueh et al.$^{}68$ find a value of $4.18\,\mathrm{eV}$, matching even more closely with the theoretical 4.21 eV.  
  
#### 1. How does Nb doping affect the oxygen insertion energy, and how does that compare with theory?  
Discussion: Göbel et al.$^{}39$ studied 2 mol% Nb-doped ceria thin films and extracted an oxygen insertion energy,  
  
$$\Delta H_{\mathrm{i}} = \Delta H_{\mathrm{a-F}} - \Delta H_{\mathrm{Red}} = (-0.35 \pm 0.1)\,\mathrm{eV}.$$  
  
The authors compare this with their theoretical prediction,  
  
$$\Delta E_{\mathrm{i}} = \Delta E_{\mathrm{a-F}} - \Delta E_{\mathrm{Red}}^{\mathrm{corr}} = -0.07\,\mathrm{eV}.$$  
  
Although there is a slight difference, the result is still considered satisfactory, indicating reasonable consistency between experimental measurements and the theoretical approach.
