

#### 1. What theoretical framework and methods were used for the density functional calculations?

The calculations were performed using Density Functional Theory (DFT) within the Generalized Gradient Approximation (GGA) as formulated by Perdew, Burke, and Ernzerhof (often referred to as PBE). This approach refines the exchange-correlation functional beyond the simpler Local Density Approximation (LDA). In addition, the Projector Augmented Wave (PAW) method was used to accurately describe the interaction between core and valence electrons. These methods were implemented in the Vienna Ab initio Simulation Package (VASP), which is widely used for DFT-based material simulations.

* * *

#### 2\. What basis set was employed to expand the electronic wave functions?


The electronic wave functions were expanded using a plane-wave basis set with kinetic energies up to 500 eV. Plane-wave expansions are particularly common in solid-state calculations, offering a systematic way to control accuracy by adjusting the plane-wave cutoff energy.

* * *

#### 3\. How were the valence electrons treated for the atomic species in the system?


*   **Cerium (Ce):** A total of 12 electrons were included, corresponding to the electron configuration \[Pd\] $4f^1 5s^2 5p^6 5d^1 6s^2$.
*   **Oxygen (O):** A total of 6 electrons were included, corresponding to the electron configuration $[\mathrm{He}]\,2s^2\,2p^4$.

In a PAW framework, these electrons are treated as valence electrons, while the core electrons are described by the PAW potentials.

* * *

#### 4\. What type of supercell was used in the calculations?

A $2 \times 2 \times 2$ supercell of the cubic $Fm\overline{3}m$ fluorite structure was used. This supercell contained 96 atoms in total (which translates to 768 electrons overall). The fluorite structure is the common form for CeO$_2$, and using a supercell allows for the study of defects, doping, or other effects with reduced periodic interaction artifacts.

* * *

#### 5\. How was the Brillouin zone sampled?


The Brillouin zone integration was carried out with a $2 \times 2 \times 2$ Monkhorst-Pack grid in the first Brillouin zone of the cubic supercell. Monkhorst-Pack grids are a systematic way to sample reciprocal space for plane-wave-based DFT calculations, balancing computational cost and accuracy.

* * *

#### 6\. What were the convergence criteria for structural relaxations?

Structural relaxations proceeded until the Hellmann-Feynman forces on each atom were less than $0.005\,\mathrm{eV\,\AA^{-1}}$. This ensures that the relaxed geometry is close to an energy minimum and that residual forces acting on the atoms are negligible.

* * *

#### 7\. How were localized $\mathbf{}4f$ electrons in cerium treated?

A Hubbard $U$ parameter was introduced to account for the strong on-site Coulomb interactions of localized Ce $4f$ electrons. A rotationally invariant approach was used, with an effective $U$ value of $5.0\,\mathrm{eV}$, as recommended in the literature. This approach (often referred to as DFT+U) improves the description of electronic states that exhibit significant on-site electron correlation (such as rare-earth $4f$ electrons).

* * *

#### 8\. Were there any corrections applied for extraneous interactions in the calculations?

Yes. The calculated energies were corrected for image-charge interactions using the method proposed by Freysoldt et al. (Ref. 43). Image-charge corrections are important when dealing with charged defects in periodic cells, as artifacts can arise from the electrostatic interaction of a charged defect with its periodic images.
