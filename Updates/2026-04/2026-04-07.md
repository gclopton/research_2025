Even though these runs did not finish, the table already supports one practical conclusion: `32` cores is clearly the best of the three tested settings for this system. The average real time per DAV iteration decreases monotonically from `38.85 s` at `8` cores to `26.02 s` at `16` cores to `12.04 s` at `32` cores, and the average real time per ionic step improves from about `118.5 min` to `38.9 min` to `12.8 min`. For production calculations, `8` cores appears too slow, while `32` cores is strongly preferable to both `16` and `8`.

At the same time, the table does not yet support a precise statement about parallel-scaling efficiency. The average number of DAV iterations per ionic step also changes significantly across the runs (`183.0`, `89.7`, `63.6`), so this is not a perfectly controlled comparison of identical work at different core counts. The safe inference is therefore operational rather than formal: for this `Ce7O12 1x1x1` test, `32` cores is already the best available choice among the tested options, but a fully converged set of runs would still be needed before making rigorous claims about final walltime or strong-scaling efficiency.
