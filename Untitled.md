
Swift heavy ions are energetic heavy projectiles whose energy loss in solids is dominated by electronic stopping, producing nanometer-scale latent tracks through intense localized electronic excitation [[Papers/Computational Architecture/Phase 4 Protocol Discipline, Artifacts, and Representatives/markdown_originals/Medvedev et al 2023 - Frontiers, challenges, and solutions in modeling of swift heavy ion effects in materials/Medvedev et al 2023 - Frontiers, challenges, and solutions in modeling of swift heavy ion effects in materials|(1)]]. The morphology of these tracks records how a material converts ultrafast electronic excitation into defect formation, phase transformation, and recovery [[Papers/Computational Architecture/Phase 4 Protocol Discipline, Artifacts, and Representatives/markdown_originals/Medvedev et al 2023 - Frontiers, challenges, and solutions in modeling of swift heavy ion effects in materials/Medvedev et al 2023 - Frontiers, challenges, and solutions in modeling of swift heavy ion effects in materials|(1)]]. In ceria, experiments suggest that swift-heavy-ion damage proceeds through a coupled oxygen-disorder/redox response, with oxygen redistribution, partial $\mathrm{Ce}^{4+}\rightarrow\mathrm{Ce}^{3+}$ reduction, and substantial crystalline retention in the track region [[Papers/Ceria Radiation Chemistry and Hypothesis Anchors/Section 5: Competing Mechanisms and Non-Redox Controls/markdown_originals/Cureton et al 2021 - Review of Swift Heavy Ion Irradiation Effects in CeO2/Cureton et al 2021 - Review of Swift Heavy Ion Irradiation Effects in CeO2|(2)]].

This behavior makes ceria a particularly challenging simulation target. Swift-heavy-ion track formation spans electronic excitation, energy transport, transient lattice heating, and picosecond recovery across disparate length and time scales [[Papers/Computational Architecture/Phase 4 Protocol Discipline, Artifacts, and Representatives/markdown_originals/Medvedev et al 2023 - Frontiers, challenges, and solutions in modeling of swift heavy ion effects in materials/Medvedev et al 2023 - Frontiers, challenges, and solutions in modeling of swift heavy ion effects in materials|(1)]]. In ceria, oxygen-vacancy formation is further coupled to localization of electrons in Ce $4f$ states, so fixed-charge interatomic potentials cannot represent the evolving mixed-valence chemistry, while direct $\mathrm{DFT}+\mathrm{U}$ remains too expensive for nanometer-scale track simulations [[Papers/DFT + U Reference Data and MLIP Training/Section 1: DFT+U Methodology for f-Electron Oxides (Metastability, Convergence, Branch Control)/markdown_originals/Fabris et al 2005 - Taming multiple valency with density functionals; A case study of defective ceria/Fabris et al 2005 - Taming multiple valency with density functionals; A case study of defective ceria|(3)]], [[Papers/DFT + U Reference Data and MLIP Training/Section 1: DFT+U Methodology for f-Electron Oxides (Metastability, Convergence, Branch Control)/markdown_originals/Loschen et al 2007 - First-principles LDA+U and GGA+U study of cerium oxides; Dependence on the effective U parameter/Loschen et al 2007 - First-principles LDA+U and GGA+U study of cerium oxides; Dependence on the effective U parameter|(4)]], [[Papers/Ceria Radiation Chemistry and Hypothesis Anchors/Section 6: Theoretical Frameworks for Coupled Redox-Thermal Track Models/markdown_originals/Darkins and Duffy 2018 - Modelling radiation effects in solids with two-temperature molecular dynamics/Darkins and Duffy 2018 - Modelling radiation effects in solids with two-temperature molecular dynamics|(5)]]. This raises a central question: are track formation and annealing in ceria governed entirely by thermal-spike mechanics, or does redox chemistry actively reshape the defect landscape during the spike and quench?

We develop a multiscale framework combining $\mathrm{DFT}+\mathrm{U}$, a redox-capable machine-learning interatomic potential (MLIP), and two-temperature molecular dynamics (TTM-MD) [[Papers/Ceria Radiation Chemistry and Hypothesis Anchors/Section 6: Theoretical Frameworks for Coupled Redox-Thermal Track Models/markdown_originals/Darkins and Duffy 2018 - Modelling radiation effects in solids with two-temperature molecular dynamics/Darkins and Duffy 2018 - Modelling radiation effects in solids with two-temperature molecular dynamics|(5)]], [[Papers/DFT + U Reference Data and MLIP Training/Section 3: Training Corpus Design for Reactive and Redox-Active Oxides/markdown_originals/Stippell et al 2024 - Building a DFT+U machine learning interatomic potential for uranium dioxide/Stippell et al 2024 - Building a DFT+U machine learning interatomic potential for uranium dioxide|(6)]]. Preliminary TTM-MD simulations with a classical rigid-ion potential reproduce transient heating and point-defect production but cannot capture the chemically realistic response suggested by experiment, because vacancy-rich environments are constrained to a fixed-charge $\mathrm{CeO}_2$ energy surface. To overcome this limitation, we calibrate a consistent $\mathrm{DFT}+\mathrm{U}$ reference across the $\mathrm{CeO}_{2-x}$ reduction series ($\mathrm{CeO}_2$, $\mathrm{Ce}_7\mathrm{O}_{12}$, and $\mathrm{Ce}_2\mathrm{O}_3$) and use it to generate the strained, defective, and finite-temperature training configurations for an Allegro neural network potential [[Papers/DFT + U Reference Data and MLIP Training/Section 1: DFT+U Methodology for f-Electron Oxides (Metastability, Convergence, Branch Control)/markdown_originals/Loschen et al 2007 - First-principles LDA+U and GGA+U study of cerium oxides; Dependence on the effective U parameter/Loschen et al 2007 - First-principles LDA+U and GGA+U study of cerium oxides; Dependence on the effective U parameter|(4)]], [[Papers/DFT + U Reference Data and MLIP Training/Section 1: DFT+U Methodology for f-Electron Oxides (Metastability, Convergence, Branch Control)/markdown_originals/Fabris et al 2005 - Taming multiple valency with density functionals; A case study of defective ceria/Fabris et al 2005 - Taming multiple valency with density functionals; A case study of defective ceria|(3)]].

The resulting MLIP is designed to represent oxidized, mixed-valence, and reduced ceria within large-scale TTM-MD simulations. A redox-capable versus fixed-charge comparison will quantify whether cation redox flexibility changes predicted track radii, defect populations, and early recovery kinetics, and more broadly whether redox chemistry can serve as a design variable for radiation tolerance in fluorite oxides.
