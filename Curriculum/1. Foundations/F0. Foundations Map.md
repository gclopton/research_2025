> [!abstract] Purpose
> The prerequisite layer beneath the research curriculum ([[00. Master Curriculum Map]]). These are standard upper-undergraduate and early-graduate courses that make the research tier learnable without constant prerequisite backfilling. The inclusion test: **a course belongs here only if a research module silently assumes it.** 33 courses across six areas.

# How to Use the Foundations Tier

## The Diagnostic Scheme (not A/B/C)

Foundations priority is not about how much of a course is relevant — it is about what is already known. Every course starts at **Assess** and moves to exactly one of:

- **Skip** — assessment (attempting representative problems, not re-watching lectures) shows solid command.
- **Targeted Review** — specific gaps identified; study only those topics.
- **Full Course** — genuine first contact or deep gaps; work the course properly.

Record the outcome in the course heading and in the Course Index below. This tier is a checklist to retire, not a parallel curriculum.

## Conventions

1. **Single combined courses.** No I/II sequences — one course per subject (e.g., one Quantum Mechanics course). Where combining risks dropping standard back-half material, the course's required-content list names it explicitly (e.g., scattering theory inside Quantum Mechanics).
2. **One subject, one home.** Thermodynamics lives once (F1), probability/statistics once (F3), numerical methods once (F5); other areas cross-link instead of duplicating.
3. **Same resource structure as the research tier.** Each course carries the six resource subsections defined in [[00. Master Curriculum Map]], with the same table formatting rules.
4. **Required-content lists are the acceptance test.** A candidate course that misses required content needs supplementing from another source — same harvest-selectively logic as the research tier.

## The Area Set

- [[F1. Physics]] — mechanics, electrodynamics, quantum mechanics, atomic physics, thermal/statistical physics, intro solid state. *(9 courses)*
- [[F2. Thermal, Fluids, and Continuum Physics]] — fluids, heat transfer, transport phenomena, intro continuum mechanics. *(4 courses)*
- [[F3. Mathematics]] — practical, physics-facing math from linear algebra through math methods; real analysis as the optional theory-depth addition. *(9 courses)*
- [[F4. Chemistry and Materials]] — physical/quantum/inorganic chemistry and the materials-science survey. *(4 courses)*
- [[F5. Computation]] — programming, algorithms, intro numerical methods, computational physics, computing craft. *(5 courses)*
- [[F6. Nuclear and Radiation]] — nuclear science language and radiation detection. *(2 courses)*

There is deliberately **no machine-learning foundation area**: research modules 7.1 (Statistical Learning Fundamentals) and 7.2 (Deep Learning) already sit at entry level, and the statistics they assume lives in F3.7.
