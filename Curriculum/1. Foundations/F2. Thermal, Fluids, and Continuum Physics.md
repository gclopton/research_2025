# F2. Thermal, Fluids, and Continuum Physics

### F2.1 Fluid Mechanics — Assess

**Why it's here:** Valuable less because the material flows than because it teaches conservation laws, diffusion/advection, nondimensionalization, constitutive assumptions, and shocks — the modeling discipline behind research area 05.

**Required content:**

- Continuum hypothesis; Eulerian and Lagrangian descriptions.
- Euler and Navier–Stokes equations; viscous flow basics.
- Dimensional analysis and nondimensionalization.
- Compressible flow and shock waves at an introductory level.

### F2.2 Heat Transfer — Assess

**Why it's here:** Directly relevant to thermal spikes (5.7) and heat conduction (5.2).

**Required content:**

- Steady and transient conduction; thermal resistance.
- Convection at a basic level; radiation at a basic level.
- Lumped-capacitance and fin models.
- Introductory numerical conduction (explicit/implicit schemes).

### F2.3 Transport Phenomena — Assess

**Why it's here:** The unified momentum/heat/mass framework (BSL-style) that research 5.3 and 5.4 generalize.

**Required content:**

- Constitutive laws: Newton, Fourier, Fick; the transfer analogies.
- Diffusion and advection; characteristic times.
- Boundary layers at an introductory level.
- Interphase transport and balances.

**Note:** If a strong unified transport-phenomena course is used, it can absorb much of the fluid-mechanics and heat-transfer content above — choose one path rather than taking all three in full.

### F2.4 Continuum Mechanics — Assess

**Why it's here:** Introductory tensor-based mechanics so the graduate treatment (research 5.1) starts on familiar ground.

**Required content:**

- Index notation and Cartesian tensors (the declared home for tensor algebra).
- Stress and strain; principal values.
- Constitutive relations; linear elasticity intro.
- Balance laws in integral and differential form.
