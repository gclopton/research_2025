# F1. Physics

### F1.1 Classical Mechanics — Assess

**Why it's here:** Molecular dynamics is Hamiltonian mechanics made computational (research 4.4, 4.7); statistical mechanics (1.8) is built on phase space; normal modes are the phonon module (1.7) in embryo; variational methods (3.3) extend the action principle.

**Required content:**

- Newtonian mechanics; conservation laws and symmetries; central-force motion.
- Lagrangian and Hamiltonian formalism; phase space; Poisson brackets at a working level.
- Small oscillations and normal modes (do not skim — this is the phonon on-ramp).
- Introductory calculus of variations (action principle, Euler–Lagrange).
- Special-relativity kinematics (energy–momentum relations for ion kinematics in research 6.1–6.2).

### F1.2 Waves and Oscillations — Assess

**Why it's here:** Feeds phonons (1.7), stress waves and high-rate response (5.8), and the wave language of dielectric response (1.10).

**Required content:**

- Driven and damped oscillators; resonance; quality factor.
- Coupled oscillators and normal modes; beats.
- The wave equation; dispersion; phase and group velocity; standing waves.
- Fourier decomposition of wave motion; acoustic waves in media.

### F1.3 Electrodynamics — Assess

**Why it's here:** Stopping power (6.2, 6.5), dielectric response (1.10), Ewald electrostatics (4.7), and Born effective charges / LO–TO splitting (1.7) all lean on it — with the dielectrics-in-matter portion carrying the most weight for this program.

**Required content:**

- Electrostatics and boundary-value problems; multipole expansion.
- Dielectrics, polarization, and fields in matter (emphasize heavily).
- Magnetostatics; Maxwell's equations; electromagnetic waves in vacuum and in media.
- Energy and momentum of fields; radiation basics; relativistic formulation at a light level.

### F1.4 Quantum Mechanics — Assess

**Why it's here:** Non-negotiable: the entire research Physics area (01) and DFT (1.2) sit on it. A single combined course per vault convention.

**Required content:**

- Wave mechanics, postulates, operators, and Dirac notation; bound states; harmonic oscillator.
- Angular momentum and spin; hydrogen atom.
- Time-independent and time-dependent perturbation theory; variational method.
- Identical particles and exchange symmetry.
- Scattering theory: cross sections, Born approximation, partial waves — REQUIRED. Combining QM into one course must not drop this back-half material; it is the prerequisite for the cross-section language of the entire research Radiation area (06).

### F1.5 Atomic Physics — Assess

**Why it's here:** The standard course that makes f-electron physics (1.5) and multiplet spectroscopy (8.4) learnable; under-taught in most curricula and needed cold for lanthanide work.

**Required content:**

- Many-electron atoms; central-field approximation.
- Term symbols; Hund's rules; LS, jj, and intermediate coupling.
- Fine structure and spin–orbit coupling; multiplet structure.
- Selection rules; X-ray notation and core levels.

### F1.6 Thermodynamics — Assess

**Why it's here:** The single thermodynamics course of the foundations tier (dedupes the draft's separate "Thermal Physics" and continuum-area "Thermodynamics"). Research 2.7 and 5.4 assume it; doc 00's routing says never to retake it at research level.

**Required content:**

- Laws of thermodynamics; entropy; reversibility.
- Thermodynamic potentials; Maxwell relations; Legendre transforms.
- Open systems and chemical potential.
- Phase equilibrium at an introductory level; ideal and real gases.

### F1.7 Statistical Mechanics — Assess

**Why it's here:** Non-negotiable: the ramp to research 1.8 and to every sampling method in area 04.

**Required content:**

- Microstates, entropy, and the Boltzmann distribution.
- Partition functions; connection to thermodynamic potentials.
- Ensembles at an introductory level.
- Ideal gases; simple solids (Einstein and Debye models); introductory quantum statistics.

### F1.8 Introductory Solid-State Physics — Assess

**Why it's here:** A fast, Kittel-level first pass so that the graduate treatment (research 1.1) lands on prepared ground.

**Required content:**

- Crystal structures and the reciprocal lattice.
- Phonons at an introductory level.
- Free-electron model; introductory band theory.
- Semiconductors at an introductory level.

**Note:** Keep this deliberately light: research 1.1 is the real course. If assessment shows solid footing, skip straight to 1.1.

### F1.9 Plasma and Charged-Particle Physics — Optional

**Why it's here:** Optional but pointed: Lindhard stopping theory (research 6.2, 6.5) is essentially the dielectric response of an electron plasma.

**Required content:**

- Plasma oscillations; plasma frequency.
- Debye screening.
- Dielectric function of the electron gas.
- Charged-particle motion in fields.
