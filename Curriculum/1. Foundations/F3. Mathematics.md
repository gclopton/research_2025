# F3. Mathematics

### F3.1 Linear Algebra — Assess

**Why it's here:** The direct prerequisite for research 3.1 and for every matrix method in area 04.

**Required content:**

- Vector spaces, bases, and linear maps.
- Eigenvalues, eigenvectors, and diagonalization.
- Inner products, orthogonality, and projections.
- Symmetric/Hermitian matrices; quadratic forms; SVD at first exposure.

### F3.2 Vector Calculus — Assess

**Why it's here:** Assumed everywhere; div/grad/curl and the integral theorems feed every transport module in area 05 and all of electrodynamics.

**Required content:**

- Gradient, divergence, curl; line and surface integrals.
- Divergence and Stokes theorems.
- Curvilinear coordinates (cylindrical and spherical — the track geometry).
- Scalar and vector fields; flux arguments.

### F3.3 Ordinary Differential Equations — Assess

**Why it's here:** Assumed by PDEs (research 3.6), numerical ODEs (4.4), and dynamical systems (3.9).

**Required content:**

- First- and second-order linear ODEs; systems of ODEs.
- Series solutions; special points.
- Laplace-transform methods.
- Qualitative behavior: equilibria and stability at first exposure.

### F3.4 Partial Differential Equations — Assess

**Why it's here:** Foundation-level PDEs so the graduate treatment (research 3.6) and numerical PDEs (4.5) are not first contact.

**Required content:**

- Heat, wave, and Laplace equations.
- Separation of variables; eigenfunction expansions.
- Boundary and initial conditions.
- D'Alembert and fundamental solutions at first exposure.

### F3.5 Complex Variables — Assess

**Why it's here:** The most common genuine gap for materials people: silently assumed by Green's functions (3.5), many-body theory (1.3), and dielectric response (1.10).

**Required content:**

- Analytic functions; Cauchy–Riemann conditions.
- Contour integration; Cauchy's theorem and integral formula.
- Residues and pole calculus.
- Branch cuts; analytic continuation at a working level.

### F3.6 Fourier Analysis — Assess

**Why it's here:** Reciprocal space is the working language of the entire research tier.

**Required content:**

- Fourier series and transforms; convolution.
- Discrete Fourier transform at first exposure.
- Parseval relations; delta functions operationally.
- Applications to ODEs/PDEs and signal decomposition.

### F3.7 Probability and Statistics — Assess

**Why it's here:** The single probability/statistics course of the foundations tier (the draft duplicated it in an ML area; the entry-level ML itself is research 7.1). Feeds 3.7 and all of area 07.

**Required content:**

- Random variables, distributions, expectation, variance.
- Law of large numbers and central limit theorem.
- Estimation, confidence intervals, hypothesis testing.
- Linear models and regression; introductory Bayesian inference (absorbs the draft's "Linear Models and Regression").

### F3.8 Mathematical Methods for Physicists — Assess

**Why it's here:** The integrator course (Boas/Arfken level) that stitches the tools together the way physics actually uses them — and the declared home for content the named courses can miss.

**Required content:**

- Special functions and Sturm–Liouville theory — REQUIRED: Bessel functions (cylindrical track problems in research 3.5, 5.7, 6.7), Legendre functions and spherical harmonics (everything f-orbital).
- Cartesian tensor algebra (shared home with F2 Continuum Mechanics).
- Calculus of variations at an introductory level.
- Series, asymptotic thinking, and order-of-magnitude estimation at first exposure.
- Green's functions at first exposure.

**Note:** A strong math-methods course can substitute for much of Complex Variables and Fourier Analysis depth; conversely, if those are taken in full, use this course only for its unique content (special functions, tensors, variations).

### F3.9 Real Analysis — Optional

**Why it's here:** Optional, recommended for theory depth: nothing in the research tier requires it, but research 3.2 (functional analysis) becomes recipe-following without it. This is the honest 'heavy theory person' addition.

**Required content:**

- Rigorous limits, continuity, and convergence of sequences and series.
- Uniform convergence; interchange of limits.
- Riemann integration; introduction to metric spaces.
- Point-set topology of the real line at a working level.
