# 2. Materials Science and Solid-State Chemistry

*Materials science and solid-state chemistry are combined because, for this program, they are deeply interwoven: oxygen nonstoichiometry, defect equilibria, rare-earth oxide chemistry, phase stability, crystallography, and ionic transport cannot be cleanly divided without continual duplication.*

## 2A. Structure and Chemical Organization

### 2.1 Crystallography, Reciprocal Space, and Symmetry — A

*Typical course titles:* Crystallography; Structure of Materials; Crystal Symmetry and X-Ray Diffraction; Introduction to Reciprocal Space.

**Core content:**

- Bravais lattices; crystal systems; point and space groups; symmetry operations; Wyckoff positions; site symmetry.
- Reciprocal lattices; Miller indices; structure factors; systematic absences.
- Group–subgroup relations; supercells and symmetry reduction; symmetry-adapted distortions.
- Local versus average symmetry; occupational and displacive disorder; vacancy superstructures.

**Known leads:** NPTEL — *Structure of Materials*; *Crystal Symmetry, X-Ray Diffraction and Physical Properties*; *Introduction to Reciprocal Space*.

### 2.2 Solid-State Chemistry of Inorganic Materials — B

*Typical course titles:* Solid State Chemistry; Inorganic Solid-State Chemistry; Chemistry of Materials.

**Core content (extract):**

- Ionic, covalent, and metallic bonding in solids; radius-ratio ideas and their limitations; coordination polyhedra; Pauling rules as qualitative tools; Madelung energy.
- Common oxide structures; structure–composition relationships.
- Mixed valence; charge transfer; nonstoichiometric compounds; redox-active lattices.
- Topotactic transformations; structure–property relationships in inorganic solids.

**Known leads:** NPTEL — *Solid State Chemistry*.

### 2.3 Crystal Chemistry of Ceramics and Rare-Earth Oxides — C

*Assemble from:* ceramic-science courses, rare-earth monographs, and specialized reviews (a conventional course likely does not exist).

**Core content:**

- Fluorite and defect-fluorite structures; bixbyite and sesquioxide structures; pyrochlores and related fluorite derivatives.
- Rare-earth ionic-radius trends; oxygen coordination environments.
- Cerium oxide reduction sequences; ordered vacancy phases; CeO₂₋ₓ crystallographic models; local versus long-range vacancy order.
- Relationships among CeO₂, the intermediate oxides, and Ce₂O₃; structural consequences of Ce⁴⁺ → Ce³⁺.

## 2B. Defects and Nonstoichiometry

### 2.4 Defects in Crystalline Solids — A

*Typical course titles:* Defects in Materials; Defects in Crystalline Solids; Physics of Crystal Defects.

**Core content:**

- Vacancies, interstitials, antisites, and substitutions; electronic defects; dislocations at an interpretive level.
- Point-defect formation energies; defect charge states.
- Elastic and electrostatic defect interactions; Frenkel and Schottky disorder; defect complexes and association; defect-induced strain.
- Finite-temperature defect populations; defect migration.
- Irradiation-created nonequilibrium defects.

**Known leads:** NPTEL — *Defects in Materials*; *Defects in Crystalline Solids*.

### 2.5 Defect Chemistry and Nonstoichiometric Oxides — A

*Typical course titles:* Defect Chemistry; Defect Chemistry of Solids; Nonstoichiometric Compounds; Point Defect Equilibria.

**Scope note:** this is the targeted replacement for taking all of conventional electrochemistry.

**Core content:**

- Kröger–Vink notation; site, mass, and charge balances; mass-action relations; electroneutrality.
- Defect equilibria; Brouwer diagrams.
- Oxygen chemical potential; dependence on pO₂ and temperature; reduction and oxidation reactions.
- Electronic compensation; ionic compensation; defect association.
- Dilute versus concentrated-defect regimes; interacting defects; limits of formal charge assignments.

### 2.6 Solid-State Ionics and Mixed Ionic-Electronic Conductors — B

*Typical course titles:* Solid State Ionics; Ionic Conductivity in Solids; Mixed Ionic-Electronic Conductors; Electroceramics.

**Core content (extract):**

- Ionic carriers in crystals; vacancy and interstitial transport; electronic carriers and polarons.
- Ionic and electronic conductivity; Haven and correlation factors; chemical versus tracer diffusion.
- Transference numbers; ambipolar transport; chemical capacitance; electrochemical potential; space-charge layers.
- Defect-controlled conductivity; oxygen transport in fluorites; coupling between redox state and ionic mobility.

**Omit:** most battery architecture, liquid-electrolyte chemistry, cyclic voltammetry, electrode manufacturing.

## 2C. Thermodynamics, Diffusion, and Transformations

### 2.7 Thermodynamics of Materials and Chemical Potentials — A

*Typical course titles:* Thermodynamics of Materials; Advanced Metallurgical Thermodynamics; Computational Thermodynamics.

**Core content:**

- Gibbs, Helmholtz, and grand potentials; partial molar quantities; chemical potentials; activities and nonideality.
- Multicomponent equilibrium; open versus closed systems; phase equilibrium; common-tangent constructions.
- Stability and metastability; mixing free energies.
- Configurational, vibrational, electronic, and magnetic contributions.
- Oxygen reservoirs and oxygen chemical potential; temperature- and pressure-dependent phase stability.
