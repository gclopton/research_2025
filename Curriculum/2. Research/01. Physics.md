# 1. Physics

## 1A. Electronic Structure and Correlation

### 1.1 Solid-State Physics and Quantum Theory of Solids — A

*Typical course titles:* Solid State Physics; Condensed Matter Physics I; Quantum Theory of Solids; Physics of Solids.

**Core content:**

- Crystal lattices, reciprocal lattices, and Brillouin zones; Bloch's theorem.
- Free- and nearly-free-electron models; tight-binding theory; band formation and band gaps; density of states.
- Fermi surfaces at a working level; metals, insulators, and semiconductors.
- Localized versus extended states; Wannier and Bloch representations; screening in solids.
- Elementary phonon theory.

**Omit:** extensive semiconductor-device engineering, superconducting devices, low-temperature transport applications.

**Known leads:** NPTEL — *Solid State Physics*, *Introduction to Solid State Physics*, *Electronic Properties of Materials*, *Bonds and Bands in Solids*.

### 1.2 Density-Functional Theory — A

*Typical course titles:* Density Functional Theory; Electronic Structure Theory; First-Principles Materials Modelling; Computational Quantum Mechanics.

**Core content:**

- Density as the basic variable; Hohenberg–Kohn theorems; Kohn–Sham construction; functional derivatives.
- Exchange and correlation; local, semilocal, meta-GGA, and hybrid approximations; spin-polarized DFT.
- Fractional occupations and ensemble ideas; self-interaction and delocalization errors; piecewise linearity and derivative discontinuities.
- Forces and stresses; periodic boundary conditions; basis sets, k-point sampling, and smearing; pseudopotentials and PAW conceptually.
- DFT limitations and validation.

**Scope note:** this is a *theory* module first — plane-wave algorithms, diagonalization, mixing, and implementation belong in module 4.8 in [[04. Computational and Numerical Methods]].

### 1.3 Many-Body Quantum Mechanics for Condensed Matter — B

*Typical course titles:* Many-Body Physics; Quantum Many-Particle Systems; Advanced Condensed Matter Theory; Many-Body Methods.

**Core content (extract):**

- Identical particles and second quantization; fermionic creation/annihilation operators; Slater determinants and occupation-number notation.
- One- and two-particle density matrices; reduced density matrices; Hartree–Fock in solids.
- Green's functions at an introductory level; screening and dielectric response; self-energy and quasiparticles.
- Basic diagrammatic interpretation; random-phase approximation.
- Conceptual foundations of GW and DMFT.

**Omit (initially):** superconductivity, relativistic quantum field theory, elaborate diagrammatic expansions unrelated to the calculations at hand.

### 1.4 Strongly Correlated Electrons and Hubbard Models — A

*Typical course titles:* Strongly Correlated Electron Systems; Hubbard Model; Correlated Electron Physics; Electronic Structure of Correlated Materials.

**Core content:**

- One-band and multiorbital Hubbard models; on-site Coulomb repulsion; Hund coupling.
- Mott versus band insulators; local moments; charge and orbital ordering.
- Mean-field decoupling; broken-symmetry solutions.
- Double counting; rotationally invariant DFT+U; Dudarev and Liechtenstein formulations; occupation matrices; metastable electronic branches.
- Hybrid functionals as an alternative treatment; conceptual relationship between DFT+U, Hartree–Fock, and model Hamiltonians.

**Scope note:** this module should explain what the VASP +U tags are doing *physically* — not merely how to set them.

### 1.5 Magnetism, Crystal Fields, and f-Electron Physics — B

*Typical course titles:* Magnetism in Solids; Rare-Earth Magnetism; Crystal Field Theory; Electronic Structure of f-Electron Systems; Lanthanide Physics.

**Core content (extract):**

- Spin and orbital angular momentum in solids; exchange interactions; direct exchange, superexchange, and double exchange.
- Crystal-field splitting; Stevens operators at a working level.
- Spin–orbit coupling; LS, jj, and intermediate coupling; multiplet structure; Kramers and non-Kramers states.
- Rare-earth local moments; 4f–5d–2p hybridization.
- Formal valence versus measurable local electronic state; Ce³⁺/Ce⁴⁺ electronic configurations.

**Omit:** magnetic-device engineering.

## Physics Scope Guard

Do **not** automatically include complete courses in: general relativity, particle physics, quantum information, superconductivity, high-energy quantum field theory, semiconductor-device fabrication, or astrophysics. They are intellectually valuable but not part of the four-year research foundation.
