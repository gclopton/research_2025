# 5. Continuum Transport and Mechanics

## 5A. Continuum Foundations

### 5.1 Continuum Mechanics and Conservation Laws — A

*Typical course titles:* Continuum Mechanics; Advanced Continuum Mechanics; Continuum Physics.

**Core content:**

- Material and spatial descriptions; kinematics; stress tensors.
- Conservation of mass, momentum, and energy; integral and differential balances.
- Constitutive equations; objectivity at a working level; small-strain mechanics.
- Field boundary conditions; coupled source terms.

**Omit (initially):** full nonlinear solid-mechanics treatment.

## 5B. Transport

### 5.2 Heat Conduction and Thermal Transport — A

*Typical course titles:* Conduction Heat Transfer; Advanced Heat Transfer; Thermal Transport in Solids.

**Core content:**

- Fourier conduction; transient heat equations; temperature-dependent properties; anisotropic conduction.
- Internal source terms; cylindrical heat flow; Green's-function solutions.
- Thermal diffusivity; boundary and interfacial resistance; phase-change enthalpy.
- Non-Fourier behavior at short scales; continuum limits of atomistic temperature.

### 5.4 Irreversible Thermodynamics and Coupled Transport — A/B

*Typical course titles:* Nonequilibrium Thermodynamics; Thermodynamics of Irreversible Processes; Coupled Transport Phenomena.

**Core content:**

- Local equilibrium; entropy production; thermodynamic forces and fluxes; Onsager coefficients.
- Coupled heat, mass, and charge transport; thermodiffusion; chemical diffusion.
- Electrochemical potentials; ambipolar transport; Nernst–Planck equations.
- Limits of linear irreversible thermodynamics.

**Scope note:** this is where the useful fraction of electrochemistry belongs *mathematically*.

## Continuum Scope Guard

Do not automatically take complete sequences in: turbulence, aerodynamics, boundary-layer fluid mechanics, viscous-flow engineering, combustion, advanced finite elasticity, structural design, or plasticity. The project needs conservation laws, diffusion, heat transport, and transient thermoelastic response — not the entire mechanical-engineering continuum curriculum.
