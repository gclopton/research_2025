# Project Overview

This project is focused on the multiscale modeling of swift-heavy-ion (SHI) irradiation in redox-active fluorite oxides, with ceria ($\mathrm{CeO}_2$) as the main model system. The central scientific question is how electronic stopping, ultrafast thermal spikes, oxygen-vacancy chemistry, and cerium redox changes couple during latent track formation and early recovery. In this framing, SHI damage in ceria is not treated primarily as an amorphization problem, but as a redox-controlled defect-chemistry problem in which oxygen nonstoichiometry and $\mathrm{Ce}^{4+}\leftrightarrow\mathrm{Ce}^{3+}$ conversion actively influence track morphology, recovery pathways, and residual disorder.

The project’s modeling strategy is to build a single, internally consistent workflow that connects electronic structure to mesoscale irradiation observables. A calibrated DFT+$U$ description of the Ce-O system is used to capture the redox physics that matter most in reduced and oxygen-deficient environments, especially $4f$ localization, vacancy energetics, and the relative placement of O($2p$), Ce($4f$), and Ce($5d$) states. That reference is then used to generate training data for a redox-capable machine-learned interatomic potential spanning $\mathrm{CeO}_2$, intermediate reduced phases such as $\mathrm{Ce}_{11}\mathrm{O}_{20}$ and $\mathrm{Ce}_7\mathrm{O}_{12}$, and the reduced end member $\mathrm{Ce}_2\mathrm{O}_3$, along with strained, finite-temperature, and disordered structures relevant to irradiation conditions.

The resulting ML interatomic potential is meant to be deployed in two-temperature molecular dynamics so that electronic energy deposition, electron-phonon coupling, thermal-spike evolution, and atomistic defect production can be treated within one framework. The broader goal is to determine when SHI irradiation in ceria produces stable latent tracks versus mostly reversible excitation, and to quantify how redox flexibility, oxygen transport, vacancy formation, and local reduction feed back on defect accumulation, crystalline retention, density profiles, and track-scale chemical zoning. A secondary but important motivation is to clarify when ceria is, and is not, a faithful surrogate for other fluorite nuclear materials such as $\mathrm{UO}_2$ and $\mathrm{ThO}_2$ under electronic-stopping irradiation.
